 These rules for the answer come first where they differ from the above. Only the first 30,000 characters of ANSWER.txt are read: keep it below that, and for a long list keep every requested row and drop links and per-row evidence first. When the task fixes the form of the answer (just a number, one name, a given format), ANSWER.txt holds exactly that and nothing else: one result, no label, explanation or link; the evidence goes in NOTES.md. A thing with several names is answered by the name most people use for it: a rarer name never wins, even where the wording of the task seems to favor it. When the task names a source, the answer is what that source's own data shows, read directly (a chart, a table, a transcript), even where other sources, its commentary or its generated summaries differ. If that source holds a value that answers the task, answer with it even if you doubt it or the wording seems to point elsewhere: do not correct it from other sources or your own reasoning. When nothing fits the task, say that first and plainly (no matching item in the period, with the search that shows it), and name a closest item only after that, as a side note. To compare statements, compare only those that make the same claim about the same thing. Report a figure as the source displays it: do not adjust, convert or normalize it unless the task asks. A limit the task states (within 15 minutes, under a price) must hold by the figure the site gives now: an item that meets it only under a favorable reading is not the answer while another item meets it outright. Every item the task asks for, for every entity it names, is required: when one is missing, keep looking or choose another option the task allows for which all of them can be found, rather than deliver a gap. A number asked for in a stated unit is given in that unit: asked how many thousand, the answer counts thousands. To name the first or earliest of several items, find each one's exact date: a citation or a shared year does not settle the order. Each field of each row is checked on that item's own primary page (a paper's own author header for affiliations), not carried over from a list or memory. When the site itself refuses the browser (a hard block or a challenge that does not clear), do not give up and do not stop at a partial result. First open in the browser the same site's other public doors: its other subdomains, section and author pages, sitemaps, feeds, and the structured data of pages it serves, and do the task there, so the evidence is the site's own and seen in the browser. Only then may web search, its cached copies, or archives fill what is left: the rule against reading the named website through them no longer holds. Say in one line where the facts came from, and deliver the full result the task asks for.
